Batch #18: Blueberry Mead

Part of Experimentation Matrix 4

Goals

To experiment with fruit meads by making a blueberry mead. This experiment is loosely based on blueberry meads from various sources including books and mead making youtube channels.

Fruit contains water, sugar and wild yeast. Complex math may yield exact gravity predictions, but that level of precision is not necessary for this experiment. Instead I'll mix a must that will yield ~13% abv at 1.000 FG and stir it in with 2 lbs of organic blueberries. The final abv should therefore be approximately 13% or some small amount lower. This batch should age for a minimum of 12 months before tasting.

Procedures

  1. Sanitize all equipment:
    1. Primary fermenter; 2 gallon carboy
    2. Airlock
    3. Hydrometer and test jar
    4. 5 gallon water jug
    5. Stirring tool
  2. Organic Blueberries - 2 lbs
    1. Lightly mash and add 1/2 teaspoon of pectic enzyme.
    2. Leave in primary for 1 - 24 hours.
  3. Ingredients:
    1. Build gradually, measuring gravity and temperature along the way.
    2. Measure ~128 oz. of water and add to primary fermenter.
    3. Add 1.5 pounds of honey to primary fermenter and stir until fully dissolved.
  4. Add dry yeast directly to primary fermenter.
  5. Place airlock on primary fermenter and store in cool, dark place.
  6. Degas, record gravity and temperature every 12 hours for the first 36 hours.
  7. Allow mead to ferment until bubbling is greater than 1 minute (approximately 2 weeks).
  8. Transfer to secondary fermenter and allow fermentation to complete.
  9. Stabilize and back-sweeten.

Ingredients

  • Organic Blueberries - 2 lbs
  • Poland Spring Water
  • 1.5 pounds Glory Bee Raw Clover Honey
  • 2 grams Lalvin ICV 41B Yeast

July 13, 2026 7:35 AM no bubbles observed, no gas released by degassing. Looks like fermentation hasn't started yet. Will check again later today.

July 14, 2026 Today's measurement indicates fermentation has started but slowly. Gave the batch a shake to see if more oxygen will help but I suspect one ore more of the following to be true. First that 2 grams of yeast may need nutrient to be more effective. Second that the yeast old and therefore less effective. Third that the addition of blueberries changes the ph of the solution thus changing the nature of yeast/nutrient needs.

What follows then is to add yeast, nutrient or both. However slow starts seem common in the absence of rehydration so I'll give the batch another 12 to 24 hours to observe the results.
