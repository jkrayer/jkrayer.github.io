Batch #17: Hydromel Strength Semi-Sweet Mead

Part of Experimentation Matrix 4

Goals

Batch 16 represented deviations in both honey and yeast preparation from batch the batch 7 procedure it was meant to replicate. Additionally I had, and wanted to pursue, the idea of back sweetening with different fruit blossom honeys a single root mead. After transfer to secondary I also found my yield wanting with more head space than I'd desire for long term aging.

The goal here is twofold. First to more closely cleave to the procedure of batch 7, excepting the difference in honey. Second to more accurately measure the starting water in order to minimize the head space of the three gallon carboy during aging.

Additionally the recipe calls for 6 pounds of liquid honey but batch 16 required only 4 pounds of crystallized honey to reach the desired starting gravity. Gravity is the north star, honey is consequential and will be measured and recorded along the way.

Procedures

  1. Sanitize all equipment:
    1. Primary fermenter
    2. Airlock
    3. Ladle for Honey Transfer
    4. Hydrometer and test jar
    5. 5 gallon water jug
    6. Stirring tool
  2. Ingredients:
    1. Build gradually, measuring gravity and temperature along the way.
    2. Measure ??? oz. of water and add to primary fermenter.
    3. Add 4.5 pounds of honey to primary fermenter and stir until fully dissolved.
  3. Add dry yeast directly to primary fermenter.
  4. Place airlock on primary fermenter and store in cool, dark place.
  5. Degas, record gravity and temperature every 12 hours for the first 36 hours.
  6. Allow mead to ferment until bubbling is greater than 1 minute (approximately 2 weeks).
  7. Transfer to secondary fermenter and allow fermentation to complete.
  8. Stabilize and back-sweeten.

  • 5 grams Lalvin ICV D47 Yeast

Observations

June 27, 2026 Poured 3 measured gallons of water into 3 gallon carboy and found it came to the exact perfect amount of head space. This leaves me to guess at the amount of loss between primary and secondary fermentation. I'd say between 16 and 32 ounces. This batch will therefore need to be 32 oz. larger to account for loss.

July 2, 2026 3 measured gallons of water plus 4.75 pounds of honey resulted in nearly 4 gallons of must. The actual volume must therefore be less than 384 oz. and greater than 336 oz. Seems like I'm splitting a fine hair here.

July 18, 2026 2:38 PM I don't see this bubbling and believe it to be stalled given the yeast, time and current gravity.

July 18, 2026 6:00 PM, Now actively bubbling again with no action on my part.

July 31, 2026 Decided to stabilize here due to the second stall in fermentation and the very long time my primary fermenter has been occupied.

What I learned here is that not prepping the yeast was a mistake. Could be the age of the yeast or the volume of the batch or temperature. In any case the next hydromel strength brew will have to have correctly prepared yeast. Also I need to experiment with something to give it body. A 5%$ bre is basically pale honey water.
